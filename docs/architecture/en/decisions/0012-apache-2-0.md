# 0012 — Apache-2.0, and the AGPL was the alternative

**Date:** 2026-08-22 · **Status:** accepted · **Decider:** Laurent Boucher

## Context

Vectispire is server software that organisations run inside their own estate, and its remote agent
is installed further still — on build infrastructure the operator does not always own. Two licences
were seriously considered, and they fail in opposite directions.

**What the AGPL would buy.** Section 13 extends the copyleft obligation to network use: an
organisation that modifies Vectispire and makes it available to users over a network must offer
those users the modified source. That clause was written for precisely this shape of product —
server software a competitor can host as a service and never contribute back to.

**What it would cost, and this is what decided it.** The obligation lands on the people the product
is for. Enterprise legal review commonly refuses AGPL components anywhere in an estate, precisely
because "users interacting remotely through a network" is hard to bound once the software is
internal-facing and the agent has been pushed onto somebody else's CI. A security tool that legal
will not approve is never evaluated, whatever it detects — and the buyers here *are* the
organisations with that policy.

There is a second argument, smaller, and specific to this product rather than to licences in
general. Vectispire evaluates other people's licence policy: [`LicenseBlocklist`](../../../../vectispire-java/vectispire-common/src/main/java/com/asmolabs/vectispire/common/domain/licenses/LicenseBlocklist.java)
reports the components whose licence appears on the operator's forbidden list. The AGPL is the
entry most likely to be on that list. Shipping the evaluator under the licence its own users most
often forbid is not fatal, but it is a conversation at every adoption.

**Why not MIT or BSD**, since permissive-versus-copyleft was not the only axis. Apache-2.0 §3 is an
express patent grant from every contributor, terminating for anyone who brings a patent claim over
the work. AGPL-3.0 §11 carries an equivalent — so patents do not separate Apache from the AGPL;
they separate both from the short permissive licences, and that is why neither MIT nor BSD-3 was
chosen.

## Decision

Apache License 2.0, with a `NOTICE` file, for the whole repository — control plane, agent and
interface alike.

## Consequences

**A competitor may host Vectispire as a service and contribute nothing back.** That is the price,
it is not hypothetical, and nothing in the chosen licence prevents it. It was accepted because
adoption inside enterprises is what this project needs first, and the AGPL trades exactly that
away.

**Contributions arrive under the same grant.** Apache-2.0 §5 makes an inbound contribution carry
the same terms, patent grant included, without a separate CLA to administer.

**Third-party components must stay compatible.** Apache-2.0 absorbs MIT, BSD and ISC; it cannot
absorb GPL or AGPL code into the distributed artefacts. The scanners are unaffected by that
constraint and it is worth saying why rather than leaving a reader to wonder: they are separate
programs, pulled as pinned container images and invoked across a process boundary by
[`ContainerRunner`](../../../../vectispire-java/vectispire-common/src/main/java/com/asmolabs/vectispire/common/scanning/ContainerRunner.java),
not libraries linked into the jar. Each keeps its own licence and its own distribution.

**When to revisit.** If the project's centre of gravity moves from adoption to a hosted commercial
offering, the calculation inverts: the clause that keeps a competitor honest becomes worth more
than the enterprises it excludes. Not before — and a change would not be retroactive over versions
already published.
