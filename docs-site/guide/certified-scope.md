# Certified scope

What your management system's certified scope covers, and how much of it carries current
evidence.

## Why this screen exists

**A tool that measures its own coverage always reports one hundred per cent.** Every other screen
answers a question about the targets somebody registered here. A management system has a scope
declared in a document, and the two are not the same set.

An estate scanned in full, made of half the certified assets, reads as a clean result — and no
query inside the product can notice it. That is the gap this screen is for.

## The number at the top is about what is missing

"Your scope names forty assets, this instance holds thirty-one" is the sentence an audit begins
with. It cannot be derived: the declared count is copied from your scope document into
**Settings → Certified scope**, alongside the scope statement itself.

The scope statement is stored so that an evidence bundle carries the scope it describes. An
archive whose reader has to be told separately what it was about is half an archive.

## Nothing is in scope by default

A scope nobody has drawn is an **undrawn scope**, not the whole estate. Until a count is declared
the screen says *not declared* rather than producing a percentage against an unknown denominator.

That distinction matters more than it looks: zero missing assets and complete coverage produce the
same number, and telling them apart is this screen's entire value. An undeclared scope reports no
gap for the same reason an empty room reports no noise.

## Fresh evidence, against the declared total

The coverage share is computed against the **declared** count, never against what the instance
holds. Dividing by what you have is exactly how a tool announces one hundred per cent over a tenth
of an estate.

"Fresh" is governed by the compliance freshness window in
[Settings](../administration/settings.md): past that many days a scan stops counting as a current
observation, and a control assessed on incomplete coverage becomes *not assessed* rather than
compliant.
